Two candidate fixes evaluated against run data and rejected on measurement:

**Weak-anchor expansion gate** — proposed to stop expansion pile-on on negative queries (~18→24 rows). Top-direct-relevance distribution overlaps completely: negatives anchor at 0.56–0.80, the co-change *winners* anchor at 0.50–0.68 — any strength threshold kills commit wins before touching negatives. The actual `neg_ok` regression decomposed to 4 silence-gate boundary flips (code_gradient wobbling across ~0.005 via index-state drift between corpus snapshots) — all-direct results, structurally impossible for expansion channels to cause. Pre-existing gate weakness, documented as 'batch-composition noise' in the report card.

**Corroborated-expansion promotion** — proposal: elevate expansions reachable via ≥2 independent channels into directs to lift MRR/P@5. Offline corroboration check: **0/52** expansion-only expected hits have a second channel — all are co-change only (co-change finds exactly what nothing else reaches, which is why it works). A ≥2-channel rule has nothing to promote; single-channel promotion was already measured negative twice.

Flat P@5/MRR on commit queries is the intentional direct/expansion boundary — hits live in the expansion tail by design (weak-evidence context for vocabulary-gap queries). Not an ordering bug.
