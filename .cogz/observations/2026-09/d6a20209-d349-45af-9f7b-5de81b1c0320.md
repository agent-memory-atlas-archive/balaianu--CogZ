Expanded `benchmark/nli_compare/pairs.jsonl` to 184 pairs (69C/67E/48N,
built from design docs incl. numeric/mechanism/direction contradiction
types) and benchmarked 6 candidates on CPU ONNX, unpadded.

Results (accuracy / p50 / model size):
- tasksource-small (deberta-v3-small + 600-task mix): .571, 39ms, 172MB
- sileod/deberta-v3-xsmall-tasksource-nli: .533, 30ms, 87MB
- cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-xsmall (current): .489, 35ms, 87MB
- cross-encoder/nli-MiniLM2-L6-H768: .435, 28ms, 83MB
- laurer-large-anli: .333, 225ms, 622MB
- laurer-base-mnli-fever-anli: .286 — predicted neutral on ALL pairs

Findings:
- Training mix dominates: tasksource (fact-verification + logic tasks)
  beats vanilla SNLI/MNLI; ANLI-trained models collapse to abstention on
  spec text regardless of size. Laurer line is out.
- xsmall-tasksource is the drop-in upgrade: same 87MB footprint, same
  sentencepiece/deberta-v3 contract, faster than current, +4.4pp.
- tasksource-small adds +3.8pp more at 2x size — quality-tier option.
- Export recipe validated: legacy torch.onnx.export (dynamo=False) +
  quantize_dynamic excluding encoder-layer Gather nodes reproduces the
  official prepackaged ONNX byte-for-behavior (identical accuracy).
  Quantizing those gathers causes ~5s/pair (per-call dequant).
- Gotcha: tasksource tokenizers ship padding-to-1680 baked into
  tokenizer.json — must disable padding at load or latency is ~150x.
- Remaining weak class everywhere: numeric-precision contradictions
  ("threshold 0.85" vs "0.5") read as neutral; and entailment precision
  is low for all models on rule-style text.
